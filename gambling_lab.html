Experiment 3

Does doubling down recover your losses?

You just lost a fictional $10 wager. For the next wager you can either stay with $10 or increase your stake to $20. Assume the probability of winning is still 50% and the odds remain −110.

Choose a strategy and reveal the outcome.

Expected loss on next bet

Increasing the stake changes the size of the possible outcomes, not the 50% win probability or the bookmaker's margin. A loss on the previous bet does not change the next bet's odds.

Homework

What to submit

Use results from the exercises, but answer in your own words. You may submit the answers through Canvas as instructed by your professor. These are reasoning questions, not a contest to win simulated money.

  1. At −110 odds and a 50% win probability, calculate the expected value of a $100 wager. Show your calculation.
  2. Run a 50-bet simulation with a 55% win probability. Report one realized profit or loss and compare it with the expected profit. Explain the difference.
  3. After losing $10, compare keeping the next stake at $10 with doubling it to $20. What changes and what does not?
  4. Use the quarterback example to identify the condition under which new information could create a positive-expected-value wager at the offered odds.
